Ruling: SAC's 2028 1st and the five picks tied to it

Memo2026-01
DateSeptember 30, 2026
FromLeague Office
ToAll teams — SAC, MIA, MEM, MIL, DAL and CHA in particular

Six 2028 first-round picks are tied together by swap rights: SAC's, DAL's, MIA's, PHX's, MEM's and CHA's. They were traded across eleven deals over three years, and one of those deals was recorded in a single ambiguous line. This memo sets out the ruling on how they resolve, and the case for it.

The ruling

  1. Trades execute in the order they were made. Each trade can only convey what a team held at that moment. So a later trade takes a pick subject to every right an earlier trade already put on it.
  2. The draft-day SAC–MIA deal (June 20, 2026) created a pool, not an outright transfer. SAC put in its 2028 1st, as it stood after its own earlier swap rights. MIA put in PHX's 2028 1st. MIA takes the better of the two, and SAC takes the other. "SAC receives 2nd" means the second-best pick, not a 2nd-round pick.
  3. MEM keeps its swap right on DAL's 2028 1st from Trade 51. No later trade cancelled it.

Under this ruling each of the six teams ends up with exactly one of the six picks, whatever the draft order turns out to be.

How the picks resolve

Once the 2028 draft order is set, apply these steps in order. A lower pick number is a better pick.

StepTradeWhat happens
1Trade 78 (Feb 2023)SAC takes the better of SAC's and DAL's picks. The worse one stays in DAL's slot.
2Trade 15 (Jul 2024)SAC takes the better of its step 1 pick and MIA's pick. CHA, which now holds MIA's pick, gets the one SAC doesn't take.
3Trade 51 (Jan 2025)MEM takes the better of its own pick and whatever is in DAL's slot. The other stays in DAL's slot.
4Trades 53 and 31 (Feb and Jul 2026)MIL, which now holds DAL's slot, takes the better of that slot and CHA's pick. DAL gets the other.
5Draft-day deal (Jun 2026)MIA takes the better of SAC's step 2 pick and PHX's pick. SAC gets the other.

Example 1

Say the picks land at: MIA's #5, SAC's #8, PHX's #12, MEM's #15, DAL's #20, CHA's #25.

  • Step 1: SAC takes #8 over #20. DAL's slot holds #20.
  • Step 2: SAC takes #5 over #8. CHA gets #8.
  • Step 3: MEM keeps its own #15 over #20. DAL's slot holds #20.
  • Step 4: MIL takes #20 over #25. DAL gets #25.
  • Step 5: MIA takes #5 over #12. SAC gets #12.

Result: MIA #5, CHA #8, SAC #12, MEM #15, MIL #20, DAL #25.

Example 2

Say the picks land at: CHA's #3, MEM's #6, SAC's #10, DAL's #14, MIA's #22, PHX's #28.

  • Step 1: SAC takes #10 over #14. DAL's slot holds #14.
  • Step 2: SAC keeps #10 over #22. CHA gets #22.
  • Step 3: MEM keeps its own #6 over #14. DAL's slot holds #14.
  • Step 4: MIL takes #3 over #14. DAL gets #14.
  • Step 5: MIA takes #10 over #28. SAC gets #28.

Result: MIL #3, MEM #6, MIA #10, DAL #14, CHA #22, SAC #28.

This example shows the one case where SAC does not get the second-best of SAC's, DAL's and PHX's picks. When PHX's pick is the worst of those three, the second-best is the worse of SAC's and DAL's picks. Trade 78 already put that one in DAL's slot, years before the draft-day deal. SAC and MIA could not trade it between themselves, so SAC gets PHX's pick instead.

The case for this ruling

  1. A team can only trade what it holds. When SAC and MIA made the draft-day deal, SAC's pick already carried SAC's own swap rights (Trades 78 and 15). DAL's pick already carried MEM's and MIL's rights (Trades 51, 53 and 31). SAC and MIA could pool what they controlled, but not what other teams had already been given.
  2. No team is made worse off by a deal it wasn't part of. MIL, MEM, CHA and DAL were not in the draft-day deal. Under this ruling, each of them gets exactly what their own earlier trades gave them. Any other reading takes something from at least one of them without their agreement.
  3. It matches how the league sheet already reads these rights. The sheet says "MEM can swap its own for the lesser of DAL's and SAC's." That only makes sense if SAC's earlier right (Trade 78, 2023) applies before MEM's (Trade 51, 2025). This ruling applies the same principle to every right in the chain.
  4. It honours the draft-day deal as written, as far as that is possible. MIA always gets at least the best of SAC's, DAL's and PHX's picks. SAC gets at least the second-best in every case except the one explained in Example 2, where that pick was never SAC's to trade. Either team can do better than that, because SAC's swap right on MIA's original pick also feeds the pool (see below).
  5. It always gives an answer. Every possible draft order resolves to one pick per team, with nothing left over and nothing claimed twice.

Two points to note

  • MIA's own original pick is in play for MIA. The draft-day wording says "best of SAC/DAL/PHX" and doesn't mention MIA's pick. But SAC's swap right on MIA's pick (Trade 15) applies before the pool. So what SAC puts into the pool is already the best of SAC's, DAL's and MIA's picks, and MIA can end up with its own original pick (as in Example 1).
  • CHA's share depends on SAC's swap right. CHA holds MIA's original pick, but SAC can swap into it. CHA ends up with whichever of the two SAC doesn't take.

Applied

The league's pick records were corrected to match this ruling on September 30, 2026. MEM's swap right is restored, and the draft-day deal is recorded as a pool. All six teams now see the same six 2028 first-round picks on their team pages, each with its place in the chain. The corrected records were checked against every possible draft order before they were saved.
